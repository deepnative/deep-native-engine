# Learn and contribute together

**Goal:** give helpful peer feedback while keeping private work private and formal review distinct. **Suggested time:** 15–20 minutes.

Learning with others can reveal a missing assumption or a better way to explain an idea. A useful peer reply says what it observed in the shared sample, asks a clear question and suggests a check. It does not claim a credential, grade, job outcome or expert authority. A qualified formal assessment requires an assigned reviewer, an agreed rubric, versioned evidence and a separate recorded decision.

Participation should be a deliberate choice. Before joining a learning circle, know who can see your post, who moderates it, how to report a problem and how to leave. Joining a group must not publish your private assignment. A reusable example or workflow needs attribution, permission from contributors and editorial review before it is shown as curated instruction. A rejected or withdrawn contribution should not appear approved.

## Practise a bounded response

Choose one invented question:

- **Exploratory:** “My event plan gives one volunteer three simultaneous tasks. How should I check it?”
- **Non-IT professional:** “The action list says Alex owns a date that was never agreed. What should I do?”
- **IT practitioner:** “The sign-up test ideas omit account-enumeration privacy. What would you add?”

Write a reply with: (1) one observation grounded in the question, (2) one suggested check, and (3) a limit on your claim. Example for the non-IT question: “Your question says no due date was agreed. Mark the date unknown and ask the team before sharing the action list. I have not reviewed the original meeting.” This helps without pretending to be the appointed reviewer.

**Self-check:** Your reply does not reveal private work, invent a decision or call peer feedback a formal score. If you want to submit your response as a reusable resource, record attribution and rights, choose a sharing scope and wait for editorial approval. The companion community example is an invented illustration. Use only the sharing and reporting controls actually available in your space; this exercise creates no post, permission or publication decision.
