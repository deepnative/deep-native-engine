# CTP-011: synthetic entitlement ledger and completion settlement

This bounded slice implements an internal PostgreSQL accounting core for **synthetic test grants only**. It is not called by any route, offer, job or payment flow, and no live member can obtain or spend service units through it. The historical coaching allowances remain hypotheses. Foundation learning and local circle participation do not create ledger grants.

## Contract and boundaries

`syntheticLedger` accepts an explicit existing active member ID, one of five unit categories, a positive integer quantity, a caller-owned idempotency key and a required UTC validity window. Grants and new reservations require an active, unrevoked member. New reservations require the injected clock to be in the half-open interval `[startsAt, expiresAt)`. A grant holds its own available, reserved, consumed, expired and adjusted balances. A reservation names one grant, so coach minutes cannot silently substitute for review minutes or study requests. Human categories count whole minutes; the two AI/practice categories count whole requests or sessions. Each operation appends an event with an exact-request fingerprint. The same key and request return the original ID; a changed request under that key fails. Different keys against one grant serialize through a PostgreSQL row lock, and the database checks that available + reserved + consumed + expired + adjusted always equals the original grant quantity.

A reservation is settled in full, exactly once, by consume or release. Repeating the same settlement key is idempotent; a different settlement after the first is rejected. A caller explicitly invokes `expire` at or after the window end; it atomically moves unused available units to expired, stamps the grant and appends an idempotent expiry event. There is no automatic scheduler in this synthetic slice. An earlier reservation remains held through expiry and can be consumed. Releasing it at or after expiry moves the units to expired, even if the expiry operation has not yet run. Transactions roll back balance and reservation changes when event persistence fails. Database and connection errors are reduced to a fixed unavailable code, without raw SQL diagnostics. The event table rejects direct update/delete while its member exists; authorized member removal cascades the synthetic record. PostgreSQL schema and integration tests are the authority for race and rollback behavior. The unit tests cover validation, replay, failure mapping and operation paths.

An internal `adjust` operation supports only a **decrease** of currently available synthetic units before the grant expires. It never increases a grant, touches held or consumed units, changes the original quantity, or substitutes another category. The debited units move to a distinct nonnegative `adjusted` balance. The grant row lock serializes this writeoff against reservations and expiry, while an exact-key replay returns the original grant and a changed request conflicts. A failed event insert rolls the balance back. This models an invented fixture correction only: there is no staff API, correction authority, refund policy, positive adjustment or live grant.

This is an internal foundation for future approved entitlements, not an entitlement policy. No automatic grants or expiry job, policy-approved adjustment, production annual reconciliation, funded minutes, live review/support/AI settlement, staff booking, invoice, paid offer or browser journey are attached. Those areas remain on [CTP-011 #27](https://github.com/deepnative/deep-native-engine/issues/27) and its dependencies, particularly the foundation access decision in [#95](https://github.com/deepnative/deep-native-engine/issues/95). The synthetic deletion behavior must be re-evaluated against approved financial retention terms before live paid records exist.

## Invented completion bridge

The 29 September 2026 bounded #27 claim adds `settleCompletion(memberId, reservationId, key, completion)` to the internal module. Its input is an **invented completion assertion**, not a job result or proof of qualified service. No route, adapter, payment, background task, member privilege or production policy is added. Changing a background or joining a circle still cannot mint grants.

The bridge enforces these invariants:

1. A completion reference begins with `synthetic:` and contains only an alphanumeric first suffix character followed by alphanumerics, dots, underscores or hyphens, at most 120 characters total. It is an opaque fixture identifier, never member content, provider output, an invoice or a real delivery claim.
2. `review_minutes` and `support_minutes` require explicitly stated positive whole `deliveredMinutes` and nonnegative whole `preparationMinutes`; their sum must equal the reserved quantity and cannot exceed 100,000. Zero preparation is explicit. `study_requests` requires exactly `quantity: 1`. Coach minutes and mock sessions are excluded from this bridge.
3. The reservation must belong to the specified member and its grant category must match the completion. Any reservation attached to a synthetic slot hold is excluded, including an expired hold. Mismatched, missing and foreign reservations return the same fixed unavailable failure, without another member's record or balance.
4. The existing idempotency-key lock and reservation/grant row locks serialize completions, ordinary consume and release. One PostgreSQL transaction moves the entire reservation from reserved to consumed, marks it consumed, inserts an immutable completion record, and appends one consume event. The completion ID is the consume event ID and result ID; a deferred foreign key requires that event to exist at commit. No partial settlement or quantity adjustment occurs.
5. Completion references are globally unique while their synthetic history exists; reservation IDs are unique in completion history. After validating the owned reservation, a separate advisory lock serializes reference checks across grants. A reused reference cannot settle another reservation even with another key or member; it returns a fixed idempotency conflict without exposing the conflicting record. Failed inserts roll back all balance, reservation and completion changes.
6. Exact-key replay uses a snapshot of the defined completion fields, with stable field ordering, and returns the original completion ID. A changed member, reservation, reference, category, quantity or minute breakdown conflicts. Ordinary consume and completion settlement have distinct request fingerprints. A second key cannot add completion evidence to an already consumed or released reservation.
7. The established **internal accounting** rule is preserved: a previously reserved amount may be settled after grant expiry or member revocation/session expiry. This is neither a member authorization check nor permission to begin work. New reservations still require an active member/current grant; late release goes to expired units. Completion settlement never restores available units. A future live service must define its own authorization and delivery policy before using this boundary.

Migration 044 adds only `synthetic_entitlement_settlements`, its constraints, index and immutable-history trigger. Ordinary update/delete is rejected while the member exists; authorized member deletion cascades both the synthetic completion and event, so this is not a live financial retention policy. Running migration loading again is idempotent and does not fabricate completion records for older consumed reservations.

## Verification and rollback

Behavioral checks cover three learner backgrounds without automatic grants, exact-key replay/conflict, cross-member denial, category separation, one-winner last-unit concurrency, consume/release, immutable event rows, member deletion, expiry boundary/race and forced event-write rollback. The clocked tests cover Jan 31 and leap-year February boundaries. Run `make verify` on the exact commit before pushing; the shared gate also runs the existing approved local browser matrix, provisional cases, production build and dependency scan. Full-MVP coverage remains separately outstanding.

`tests/unit/ledger-settlement.test.ts` defines validation, canonical replay, caller-mutation isolation and failure behavior. `tests/integration/ledger-settlement.test.ts` checks actual PostgreSQL balances and evidence across the three learner backgrounds, preparation inclusion, cross-member/category/quantity denial, reused references, concurrent same/different-key completion, completion versus consume/release, atomic event-write rollback, late settlement after expiry/revocation, hold exclusion, and deletion/immutability. The new unit suite first failed because the bridge did not exist; passing counts and exact revision evidence are recorded separately in the delivery handoff. These internal tests add no browser journey and do not count toward full-MVP service settlement acceptance.

If rollback itself fails, the ledger discards the borrowed PostgreSQL client so an open or aborted transaction cannot be reused. The caller still receives only the existing safe ledger failure. `tests/integration/ledger-rollback.test.ts` uses a real single-connection pool with injected event-delivery and rollback failures, plus a server-rejected event insert. It checks replacement of the failed connection, released transaction locks, absence of orphan grants/reservations or balance changes, exact-key retry and subsequent success. Successful rollback remains a control that safely reuses the same connection. This is bounded synthetic reliability evidence under #42, not live financial acceptance.

The year-one integration fixture manually seeds only the historical **hypothesis** for a two-month pilot followed by ten continuation months. For Jan-31 anchors in ordinary and leap years it checks twelve contiguous half-open monthly windows, 36 category-specific grant events with exact-key replay, 720 coach minutes, 420 review minutes and 420 support minutes. It verifies that a future-month grant cannot be reserved at activation and that a changed same-key request conflicts. The pilot's one-time internal onboarding reserve is asserted separately and never appears as a second bookable grant. The CAD 3,000 plus CAD 9,000 amounts are planning inputs; the fixture makes no purchase, automatic grant, annual bundle or revenue claim. It does not prove a real continuation, qualified capacity, live settlement, price approval or production billing policy.

The expiry change adds migration 017 and extends the internal ledger module. Migration 017 fails closed for preexisting synthetic grants with no window: it records an expiry event and expires their available balance while retaining reserved units for settlement. Migration 018 adds a zero-default adjusted balance and permits immutable adjustment events; it is idempotent on the existing synthetic schema. Before any real grants, rollback is to stop calling the module, remove its synthetic fixtures and revert the migration/module through a reviewed migration. Do not drop a table containing genuine financial records; a live retention and data-migration plan requires a separate owner decision.
