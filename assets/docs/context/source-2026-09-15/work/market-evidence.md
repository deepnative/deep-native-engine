# Market evidence and Canada operating requirements

Research cut-off and access date: **2026-09-15**. Scope: remote services for individual IT contractors across roles, prices expressed in CAD. Canada-first operations are a provisional planning assumption, not a confirmed province or an exclusive customer geography. This research supports hypotheses and implementation requirements; it does not establish willingness to pay CAD 10,000–120,000 annually or certify legal compliance. The user clarified that the annual price range is per individual contractor and that the platform should serve all IT contractor roles from the beginning.

## Five decisive findings

1. **Target willing, experienced adopters with a specific work problem.** Canada's March 2026 labour-force supplement reports 39.6% of self-employed workers used generative AI at work in the prior 12 months. Among self-employed non-users, 10.7% cite missing skills or knowledge; larger shares see no applicability (48.6%) or lack interest (26.9%). Do not market to all independents as if they need or want expensive AI training. [Statistics Canada, released 2026-07-30](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260730/dq260730b-eng.htm)
2. **There is a plausible applied-skills opportunity, with no validated premium-price evidence.** MBO reports 74% of surveyed US independents prioritize continued GenAI training. Separately, among Canadian businesses using AI, 32.0% reported AI-related training for existing employees. These establish learning activity and interest, not demand for this platform or contractors' disposable training budgets. [MBO 2025 report](https://www.mbopartners.com/state-of-independence/2025-ai-report), [Statistics Canada, released 2026-06-11](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm)
3. **The feature bundle already has competition.** Pluralsight bundles courses, learning paths, labs, assessments, and an AI assistant. Aced (formerly Exponent) bundles courses, peer mocks, community, AI interview features, and optional coaching. Interview Kickstart combines technical preparation, mock interviews, and career coaching; its current price requires an inquiry. Differentiation should be role-plus-domain assignments, expert-assessed evidence, maintained workflows, and support applying them to permitted client work. That last sentence is a strategic inference, not a verified market outcome. [Pluralsight](https://www.pluralsight.com/individuals/pricing), [Aced](https://www.tryexponent.com/upgrade), [Interview Kickstart](https://interviewkickstart.com/guide/interview-kickstart-cost)
4. **Privacy and outreach rules belong in the MVP design.** Use synthetic work examples by default; require documented authorization before accepting client files; build scoped access, clear AI-processing notices, retention/deletion controls, and marketing consent/suppression records. These are recommended product controls informed by Canadian privacy principles and CASL; precise obligations depend on business and customer jurisdictions. [OPC AI principles](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/gd_principles_ai/), [CRTC consent guidance](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm)
5. **Resolve tax classification and the actual operating province before paid launch.** Training, subscription access, and coaching do not automatically receive identical tax treatment. CRA vocational-course exemptions are conditional. Registration timing has separate single-quarter and rolling-four-quarter tests at the usual CAD 30,000 small-supplier threshold. Ontario is one example where vocational-program status also warrants checking: genuine professional development for people already in the same vocation can qualify for an exemption, but remote delivery alone does not settle classification. [CRA registration](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), [CRA vocational instruction](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/20-4/vocational-schools-courses.html), [Ontario exemptions](https://www.ontario.ca/page/exemptions-registration-career-colleges)

## Launch implications and boundaries

### Customer and positioning

- Initial segment hypothesis: established independent contractors with demonstrable professional experience, a specific assignment or transition objective, capacity to practise, and client permission to use the proposed tools.
- Provide a broad role taxonomy and accessible intake from the beginning. Publish readiness for each role/domain: available, limited expert availability, or coming soon. Initial paid cohorts may span roles where qualified reviewers and complete workflows exist; no role/domain has been validated by this research, and unsupported tracks must not be sold as complete.
- Distinguish training progress, expert-assessed work quality, interview readiness, signed contracts, gross billings, retained contribution, and wellbeing/time saved. Do not claim causal earnings improvements without evidence.
- No defensible Canadian IT-contractor TAM or premium willingness-to-pay estimate was established. Use an observed prospect list and paid-pilot conversion/capacity model first. Do not multiply Canada's entire self-employed population by the proposed annual fee.
- Competitor feature presence proves alternatives exist. It does not validate a CAD 10k minimum or CAD 100k+ individual package. High tiers require separately scoped expert delivery and customer economics; treat those as experiments rather than published proven offers.

### Privacy, client material, and AI processing

- Make the first labs synthetic and portfolios private until the member explicitly shares an approved artifact.
- Collect only data needed for stated purposes. Separate learning/feedback processing from marketing and from permission to reuse an artifact in content or model improvement.
- Require client authorization where applicable; a contractor's upload confirmation cannot itself erase their client's confidentiality/IP restrictions.
- Specify what is sent to each AI or hosting provider, where it is processed, retention, and which humans can access it. Check provider contracts/settings before promising no-training or Canadian residency.
- Under the OPC's PIPEDA guidance, international processing is not categorically prohibited, but the transferring organization remains accountable. Canadian hosting alone is not a complete privacy compliance claim.
- Recommended MVP controls: least-privilege member/coach/admin access, tenant separation, secure storage and transfer, expiring share links, access/change logs, recording choice before interviews, deletion/export requests, incident response, and documented retention periods. Preserve mandatory financial records separately when a learning account is deleted.
- Exact federal/provincial application depends on location and data flows. Alberta, BC, and Quebec have substantially similar general private-sector laws; cross-border commercial flows may engage PIPEDA. Health/client-regulated data requires separate assessment rather than a generic platform promise.

### Marketing and outreach

- Acquire initial members through opted-in workshops, relevant public content, referrals, and partner introductions.
- For commercial electronic messages, record the consent or other valid legal basis, sender identity/contact details, consent source/date, and applicable expiry. Do not assume any publicly found address permits marketing.
- Provide working unsubscribe/suppression handling. CRTC guidance requires honoring opt-outs without delay and within 10 business days. Product target: immediate suppression.
- Do not let AI agents autonomously scrape addresses or send campaigns without the lawful recipient basis and reviewable campaign approval. Preparing drafts and prospect research is different from sending.

### Billing and education classification

- Prices in the business model should explicitly be **CAD before applicable taxes**. Present total payable and subscription/renewal terms clearly at checkout.
- Confirm entity, operating province, GST/HST registration, supply classification, customer location, and applicable provincial taxes before live billing. Do not hard-code 13% for every Canadian customer or assume all training is exempt.
- Support business billing names, addresses, tax registration identifiers where needed, itemized service periods, tax amount/rate/status, invoice dates, payment terms, refunds/credit notes, and accounting export.
- For taxable sales of CAD 500+ to GST/HST registrants, CRA documentation includes supplier name/date/total, tax indication and supplier registration number, buyer name, description, and payment terms. Use the full invoice fields for premium invoices rather than optimizing around minimum thresholds.
- Obtain a professional assessment of whether the planned long-form program is professional development, a vocational program, a mixed subscription, or another supply. Ontario's official page describes professional-development exemptions and a prescreening process; its rules are not assumed to apply nationally.
- Avoid claiming accreditation, government approval, tax deductibility, grant eligibility, guaranteed placement, or a recognized certification unless separately established. A portfolio assessment can be described accurately as a private platform assessment.

### Unknowns to resolve during inception

1. Entity and operating province; initial customer provinces/countries; business/tax registration status.
2. Founder expertise, existing contractor audience, client/recruiter network, qualified domain assessors, and weekly capacity.
3. First fully supported role/domain combinations within the broad intake, expert capacity for each, and whether members are upgrading within their existing vocation or preparing for a new one.
4. Actual willingness to pay, budget authority if incorporated, payment schedule, refund expectations, and financing requests.
5. Customer tool permissions, acceptable data categories, AI/hosting vendors, data locations, retention terms, and client confidentiality restrictions.
6. Whether a CAD 100k+ tier is desired as an individual high-touch advisory/implementation service, and whether individual customer economics can support it. There is no evidence here validating it.
7. Coach contracts, intellectual-property rights, insurance needs, and any future international registration/tax/consumer obligations.

## Suggested evidence statements for the final business plan

- **Verified context:** established alternatives bundle learning and interview support; Canadian data shows meaningful but uneven AI adoption.
- **Strategic inference:** integrated domain assignments, practical evidence, and maintained working tools may be more distinctive than a large generic course catalogue.
- **To validate:** specific buyer pain, niche choice, CAD prices, premium adoption, retention, delivery hours, and attributable financial benefit.
- **Do not state:** proven demand for CAD10k–120k annual individual subscriptions, guaranteed higher earnings, a validated market size, or that the plan is legally approved.
