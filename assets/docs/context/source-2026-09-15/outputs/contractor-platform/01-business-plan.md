# Contractor Success Platform — Business Plan

**Version:** 1.0 · **Planning date:** 15 September 2026 · **Currency:** CAD, before applicable taxes

**Working name:** Contractor Success Platform. This is a descriptive project name; availability of a business name, trademark, or domain has not been checked.

## 1. Executive decision

Build a platform that gives independent IT contractors a structured way to develop skills, demonstrate professional capability, use AI in their work, and manage their next stage of growth. Combine a reusable software and learning platform with clearly bounded expert services.

The initial market includes **all IT contractor roles**. Provide shared foundations, configurable role paths, and transparent expert availability. Make no claim that every role or industry has a complete training library on launch day.

Target annual subscriptions of CAD 12,000, 24,000, 48,000, and 120,000 per individual. These are proposed commercial packages, not established market prices or validated willingness to pay. Launch the CAD 12,000 offering first through a standalone CAD 3,000 pilot. Higher prices buy progressively more expert attention and defined implementation support. The CAD 120,000 offer requires an established individual consulting business, contracted delivery capacity, a specific business case, and a statement of work.

The first objective is five paying pilot members across more than one role, with useful assessed work and accurately measured delivery costs. The second objective is paid continuation and repeated usage. Building a large content library, reporting registrations, and collecting expressions of interest do not establish either objective.

## 3. Customer problem and positioning

### The problem

An independent contractor often has to arrange their own professional development, seek expert feedback, understand a new industry, maintain a marketable portfolio, prepare for interviews, and learn new AI workflows while delivering paid work. Those activities are fragmented. A course completion badge alone says little about their ability to handle a client situation.

### Proposed promise

**Help independent IT contractors build relevant skills, demonstrate them through reviewed work, and apply them with practical tools and expert support throughout their careers.**

Measure progress through completed work, better reasoning, practical tool adoption, client-ready communication, and customer-reported commercial results with their evidence status preserved.

Avoid employment guarantees, promised rate increases, implied industry accreditation, exaggerated AI productivity claims, or claims that an AI assessment predicts hiring success.

### Why the platform may be valuable

1. It organizes a contractor's learning and development around a current professional objective.
2. It uses realistic assignments to reveal gaps that generic lessons miss.
3. It connects those gaps to targeted lessons, practice, and expert feedback.
4. It gives members maintained workflows they can adapt to authorized work environments.
5. It preserves a private record of reviewed evidence, decisions, and progress.
6. It supports preparation for client conversations, interviews, renewals, and new service offers.

This is a differentiated service hypothesis. Competitors and workforce research demonstrate adjacent activity; they do not establish that customers will pay our prices.

## 4. Customer segments and role coverage

### Membership situations

| Situation | Trigger | Initial outcome | Likely service fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Currently delivering a contract | New responsibilities or tool changes | Improve capability and prepare renewal evidence | Professional |
| Preparing for a new assignment | Contract ends in 60–120 days | Reviewed portfolio, role practice, and a next-assignment plan | Pilot or Professional |
| Moving into a specialty or domain | A desired role requires credible new skills | Complete assessed work in that specialty | Specialist, once experts are available |
| Developing a personal consulting offer | Wants to sell a repeatable project | Scope, price assumptions, delivery workflow, and reference implementation | Practice |
| Running an established solo practice | Has paying clients and substantial recurring operational work | A bounded delivery-support program with expert capacity | Partner, future and by application |

Someone with an immediate income crisis may value the product but still be unable to afford it. Do not confuse need with purchasing capacity. The intake process establishes whether the offered service is useful and affordable for the buyer's stated situation, without promising a job or recommending personal debt.

### Role taxonomy at launch

- Software and application development.
- Quality assurance, test automation, and quality engineering.
- Data engineering, analytics, and applied AI.
- Cloud engineering, DevOps, and site reliability.
- Cybersecurity and identity.
- Solution, enterprise, and software architecture.
- Business analysis and business systems analysis.
- Project, program, and delivery management.
- Product ownership and product management.
- Other IT specialty, with a free-text objective and a manual fit review.

Members may choose multiple roles and a primary current objective. Do not force career identity into a single permanent label.

### Readiness labels

Every role path and domain module has one of four states: **available**, **limited coverage**, **in preparation**, or **retired**. Available means the stated content has been reviewed and the advertised expert service is bookable. Limited coverage lists exactly what is available. In-preparation material cannot be sold as delivered content. Retired versions remain traceable for historical assessment but are excluded from new recommendations.

Broad intake begins immediately. When a requested specialty lacks a qualified reviewer, offer the actual shared program and explain the gap before payment, or put the tailored service on a waitlist. Do not silently assign an unqualified generalist.

Among self-employed non-users, lack of skills was only one reason for non-use; many did not see applicability or interest. This favours serving willing adopters with concrete work needs rather than assuming everyone needs AI training. The population includes many occupations outside IT. An April 2026 research preprint identifies contextual relevance, verification effort, and credible skill signalling as challenges for freelancers learning with generative AI. ### Agent template standard

Every published workflow includes purpose, supported versions, prerequisites, input and output examples, permissions, estimated usage, tests, failure cases, owner, last verification date, license, and change history. A template may draft test cases, summarize supplied requirements, compare documents, or prepare meeting actions. Publishing a template does not authorize it to access a member's employer systems or send communications.

Launch with downloadable or copyable workflows and synthetic demonstrations. Hosted execution of arbitrary user code and automatic access to client repositories are later features requiring a separate design and launch decision.

## 7. Subscription and service design

### Annual ladder

| Plan | Annual fee | Core use | Monthly human capacity reserved per member | Launch state |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Professional | 12,000 | Ongoing development, reviewed practice, and career support | 1h coach, 0.5h domain/project review, 0.5h support | Pilot first, then paid beta |
| Specialist | 24,000 | More frequent feedback and specialty application | 2h coach, 1h domain review, 1h implementation support, 1h support | Future; capacity and demand gate |
| Practice | 48,000 | Develop and deliver a personal consulting offer | 3h coach, 2h domain review, 3h engineering, 2h support | Future; individual business case required |
| Partner | 120,000 | Substantial managed support for an established solo practice | 4h coach, 2h senior advisor, 4h domain review, 12h engineer, 8h analyst, 4h support | Future; custom statement of work |

All plans include the published shared learning library and role-path access, four pooled one-hour clinics per month, assessed progress records, mock-practice access within usage allowances, and the applicable maintained workflow library. A clinic group is capped at 16 members. Four group hours are a pooled delivery cost; they do not mean four one-to-one hours for every member. Role-specific clinics must be scheduled within an explicitly funded expert roster.

### Proposed AI usage allowances

Professional: 20 text mock sessions of up to 30 turns each and 100 study-assistant requests per billing month. Specialist: 40 sessions and 200 requests. Practice: 60 sessions and 300 requests. Partner: a negotiated allocation with a contractual maximum. Server-side token, document-size, concurrency, and cost limits apply. Failed platform requests restore the relevant entitlement; completed low-rated feedback does not automatically create unlimited retries.

These are initial product limits to test against the model's CAD 25/45/100/250 monthly AI cost assumptions. The unit cost is not verified. If the allowance cannot be delivered within budget, reduce the promised allowance before sale or change pricing. Never silently withdraw paid entitlements after enrolment.

### What justifies the premium

Professional buys a coordinated service around the member's real objective and scheduled expert attention. Specialist adds more frequent specialty feedback. Practice adds a bounded engineering resource and help productizing a personal service. Partner adds a larger managed delivery team allocation and named responsibility for agreed work packages.

The Partner plan remains unvalidated. A person who earns CAD 150,000 from a single time-and-materials contract is unlikely to find a CAD 120,000 service economically sensible. Qualification must show an established book of work and a plausible benefit materially larger than the fee. More content alone cannot create that benefit.

### Payment and term rules

- The annual service term and its full price are displayed before purchase. The baseline offers annual prepayment or 12 monthly instalments equal to the annual price divided by 12. Instalments do not imply a month-to-month agreement.
- Professional is CAD 12,000 prepaid or CAD 1,000 per month over 12 months. Future plans are CAD 2,000, 4,000, and 10,000 per month respectively, if offered on that schedule.
- Renewal requires the applicable notice and consent arrangements for the member's jurisdiction. Provide a visible cancellation-at-term-end control. Early release, hardship, provider failure, and refund requests follow an operator-reviewed policy with recorded reasons.
- These are draft commercial terms. Final consumer/professional-service terms depend on operating province, buyer location, and contract structure. Build the policy configuration and audit trail before enabling real billing.
- Tax is computed separately from revenue. The payer address, invoicing name, and any applicable registration information are collected for billing purposes.
- Do not automatically sell add-on hours or increase charges. Provide an explicit quoted add-on with member acceptance. Initial human add-ons are custom scoped; do not invent an unlimited support plan.

### Booking and service rules

The Professional coaching allocation is one 45-minute call plus 15 minutes of preparation/notes. Its 30-minute review allocation is approximately 20 minutes of review and 10 minutes of preparation. Support is a monthly time allowance, not a response guarantee for every technical issue.

Use a minimum 24-hour rescheduling window as a proposed policy. If the platform cancels, restore the service credit and offer replacement availability. A member no-show can consume the credit only after the disclosed policy is applied. Expert hours normally expire at the end of the billing period; provider-caused unavailability restores a dated replacement credit. Group recordings require the applicable participant consent. A written summary is available when recording is not appropriate.

Professional support acknowledgement target: two business days. Specialist and Practice: one business day within staffed hours. Partner: a negotiated coverage window. Acknowledgement is distinct from resolution. Default staffing hours are Monday–Friday, 09:00–17:00 Eastern time, excluding the published holiday calendar. Display times in the member's local timezone. Remote delivery does not imply 24-hour staffing.

## 8. Paid pilot and conversion

### Pilot package

CAD 3,000 for the first two billing months, with core instruction delivered over eight weeks. The pilot is a standalone purchase. No subscription conversion happens without the member's explicit acceptance.

Include:

- A baseline assessment and 90-day plan.
- The shared AI and contractor-practice foundation.
- One role-specific practical assignment using synthetic or approved material.
- Two coaching appointments and four scoped 30-minute project reviews, within the stated pilot allowance. Each review allowance includes preparation and notes.
- Text mock interviews and access to a small tested workflow library.
- Pooled weekly clinics and a closing progress review.

The pilot includes 60 support minutes in each billing month, compared with 30 minutes per month in the continuing Professional program. Its one-time onboarding capacity is 60 coach minutes and 30 support minutes. These internal delivery costs are included in the pilot budget, not billed as a second purchase. Track preparation, notes, and all service time.

Allocate onboarding to the baseline conversation, permissions, and initial plan. Use the first regular coaching appointment for the development plan and the second for the closing review and next-quarter actions. Allocate the four project reviews to baseline work, the first draft, a revision, and the final assessed artifact. Each review addresses an agreed artifact section and up to three rubric criteria within its time budget; it is not a comprehensive audit of an unlimited project. No additional closing meeting is implied.

### First-year credit

If the member continues, the CAD 3,000 pilot represents the first two months of their first CAD 12,000 membership year. They buy **the remaining ten months for CAD 9,000**, either prepaid or CAD 900 per month for ten months. The membership year starts on the pilot start date. Renewal is a new 12-month term at CAD 12,000 unless a disclosed new price is accepted.

The standard continuation is accepted by the end of the pilot and begins immediately at the start of billing month three. A late return needs a separate agreement with explicit service dates, price, and credit treatment. The platform cannot automatically bill for missed, undelivered time or promise ten remaining months before an unchanged anniversary when that time is no longer available.

Before acceptance, show that the pilot's two reviews and 60 support minutes per month become one 30-minute review and 30 support minutes per month during Professional continuation. Record the member's intended recurring uses and reassess usefulness after 90 continuation days. Good pilot feedback alone does not validate the less intensive ongoing service.

Do not sell a further 12 months for CAD 9,000, extend the first service year silently, count the pilot twice, or show CAD 15,000 as first-year revenue. If the member does not continue, only the CAD 3,000 pilot exists.

The financial model uses two calendar billing periods for this simplification. Actual invoices and service dates must use explicit start and end dates rather than assuming every month has four weeks.

## 9. Curriculum and content operations

### Shared foundation

1. Select useful AI tasks and understand model limitations.
2. Design a workflow with clear inputs, outputs, and human review.
3. Evaluate results, identify errors, and document uncertainty.
4. Understand client context, clarify requirements, and define acceptance.
5. Communicate progress, decisions, risks, and business impact.
6. Build evidence of capability and plan continued development.

### Role assignment examples

| Role | Example assessed assignment |
|---|---|
| Development | Build and explain a small integration with failure handling and tests |
| QA | Design an evidence-based test strategy and validate generated test cases |
| Data | Create a data-quality investigation with reproducible checks and a clear conclusion |
| Cloud/DevOps | Review a synthetic deployment incident and propose a tested recovery process |
| Security | Assess a synthetic access-control design and prioritize defensible improvements |
| Architecture | Compare design options, constraints, costs, and a migration sequence |
| Business analysis | Map a workflow, clarify ambiguity, and write traceable acceptance criteria |
| Project/delivery | Replan a constrained project and run a simulated stakeholder discussion |
| Product | Assess an opportunity, define a testable outcome, and prioritize a small release |

These are curriculum briefs, not claims that the courses or expert-reviewed solutions already exist.

### Domain design

Organize domain packs around workflows such as customer onboarding, claims handling, retail fulfilment, public-service case management, and enterprise identity. Start with synthetic cases. A named practitioner must review substantive domain claims before the pack is sold as available. Record source dates and local jurisdiction where relevant. A module about a regulated process is educational context, not authorization to provide regulated advice.

### Content production workflow

Brief → sourced draft → practical example → subject review → accessibility/transcript check → exercise and assessment calibration → publication → scheduled refresh. AI can prepare drafts and examples; a human owner remains accountable for published accuracy. Do not use course completion as an accredited credential. Member-facing certificates, if any, state exactly which assessed exercise was completed and by whom it was reviewed.

Refresh frequently changing workflow templates at least monthly or after a dependency-breaking change. Review slower-moving domain content quarterly. These are operating targets; missed checks mark content for review and remove unsupported current-compatibility claims.

## 11. Go-to-market and sales process

### Initial acquisition channels

Serve all roles while using clear role-specific examples in outreach and events. Test several channels at small scale:

- Founder relationships and voluntary referrals.
- Professional associations and contractor communities where participation is permitted.
- Public practical workshops with a transparent paid-program invitation.
- Expert-led portfolio review sessions.
- Useful sample scenarios and workflow demonstrations.
- Referral partnerships with coaches, training providers, and recruiters, with conflicts and compensation disclosed where applicable.

No partner relationship or referral channel is assumed to exist. No automated scraping or mass messaging is part of the initial MVP.

### Sales sequence

1. A prospect accesses an honest sample or opts into a workshop.
2. They request a fit assessment and provide a current professional objective.
3. The operator confirms role coverage, expert capacity, schedule, and suitability.
4. The prospect sees exact scope, annual context, pilot price, and conversion mechanics.
5. They choose whether to buy the standalone pilot.
6. Delivery starts only after the signed terms, payment status, and expert capacity are recorded.
7. The closing review discusses measured progress and separately offers continuation.

### First validation campaign

Planning experiment, not observed conversion: approach 40 qualified, permission-appropriate prospects across at least three role families; aim for 15 substantive fit conversations and five paid pilots. Keep source, role, objection, offered price, and outcome for each lead. These target numbers are decision thresholds, not market benchmarks.

Ask about previous spending, current alternatives, recent difficult assignments, desired results, purchase timing, and reasons for declining. A polite positive answer does not count as a sale.

### Evidence gates

Continue the Professional offer if five paid pilots can be delivered within the cost allowance, at least four finish a useful assessed assignment, and at least three purchase the ten-month continuation on disclosed terms. Failure triggers a review of scope, customer fit, price, or format rather than automatic expansion. A sample of five is directional evidence; replicate in a later cohort before claiming repeatability.

Build a low-cost materials-only subscription only if customers request it and a separate retention test supports it. It is not required to make the premium service ladder coherent and is not part of the initial pricing model.

## 12. Remote delivery and operating model

The founder owns positioning, sales quality, product priorities, expert selection, and final operating decisions. AI assists with preparation, retrieval, drafts, practice sessions, scheduling suggestions, and exception detection. The system does not independently promise professional outcomes or make contractual commitments.

Use contracted experts to cover different roles and domains. Maintain specialty evidence, conflicts, availability, loaded cost, response windows, and a backup reviewer. Do not claim that one founder can provide senior-level guidance across all IT disciplines.

Capacity is a prerequisite to accepting payment. Count preparation, notes, rescheduling, QA, support, and content maintenance. If an expert pool reaches its booked capacity, offer a later start date. Use pooled clinics for common learning while preserving the one-to-one services already promised.

## 13. Economics, funding, and cash discipline

The workbook is the calculation source for scenarios. Prices and delivery rates are planning assumptions, expressed in CAD. No salaries, contractor rates, model bills, customer acquisition costs, or conversion rates have been verified through this business.

### Unit economics assumptions

Loaded hourly costs: coach/implementation support CAD 125, domain reviewer/engineer CAD 150, senior practice advisor CAD 200, analyst CAD 80, support CAD 60. Monthly AI allowances: 25/45/100/250. Infrastructure allocation: 15/20/30/75. Payment cost assumption: 2.9% plus CAD 0.30 per successful charge. These assumptions are not a quotation from a payment provider or an expert.

At a full 16-member shared clinic group, preliminary monthly delivery-cost calculations are approximately CAD 331 for Professional, CAD 740 for Specialist, CAD 1,523 for Practice, and CAD 4,827 for Partner. These amounts include the modeled individual delivery, allocated shared clinic, AI, infrastructure, and payment costs. They exclude onboarding, acquisition, central content production, build cost, and general overhead. Smaller groups have a higher cost per member. Use the workbook for the exact calculation and active assumptions.

### Financial definitions

- **Cash collected:** actual money received before refunds, with sales tax kept separate.
- **Service revenue:** the modeled amount earned as the service is delivered. This planning schedule is not a determination of the legal accounting treatment.
- **Deferred revenue:** prepaid amounts relating to undelivered service under the model.
- **Direct contribution:** service revenue minus attributable delivery costs and payment costs.
- **Operating result:** revenue minus modeled delivery, acquisition, central operating, and build costs. The model separately states how founder compensation is treated.
- **Ending recurring run rate:** annualized continuing-member fees at the end of the model period. It is not cash collected or revenue earned during that year, and it excludes non-converted pilots.
- **Funding need:** the maximum modeled cash shortfall plus a separately chosen reserve. No funding is approved by this document.

### Founder economics and capacity

The model shows founder compensation assumptions explicitly rather than treating founder time as free. If the founder also performs modeled paid coach or reviewer work, allocate those hours once: either pay the founder through the delivery line or offset that portion of founder compensation. Do not double-count or omit it. The default forecast treats the founder as commercial/product operator and the direct service hours as purchased delivery capacity.

The initial operating constraint is expertise and attention across roles. A high-priced plan may have attractive percentage margin yet consume disproportionate scarce capacity. The Partner tier is not included as necessary revenue for a successful Professional launch.

## 14. Metrics and decision rules

| Metric | Definition | Review |
|---|---|---|
| Paid pilot acquisition | New paid pilots / qualified fit conversations, by channel and role | Each cohort |
| Activation | Member confirms goal and begins first assignment within 14 days | Weekly |
| Assessed completion | Members with reviewer-accepted final work / pilot starters | End of pilot; withdrawals remain in denominator |
| Continuation | Members who purchase remaining ten months / pilots eligible to continue | Each cohort |
| Ongoing usefulness | Continuing members using an eligible workflow, lesson, review, or clinic in the last 30 days | Monthly, with event definition fixed |
| Delivery cost | Actual expert, group, AI, support, and payment cost per active member-month | Monthly |
| Capacity | Reserved hours / available qualified hours by role and provider | Weekly |
| Review turnaround | Business days from complete submission to reviewed result | Weekly; incomplete inputs tracked separately |
| Early release/default | Approved early releases and payment losses / active members | Monthly; distinguish causes |
| Renewal | Renewals / memberships reaching the end of their term | From year two or earlier eligible anniversary cohorts |
| Commercial progress | Member-reported interviews, assignments, rate changes, or renewals with evidence labels | Quarterly; never claim causation automatically |

No aggregate success metric should expose confidential member details or turn unverified self-reports into verified placements. AI feedback quality and human review quality are measured separately.

## 15. Risk and operating controls

| Risk | Early signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Price exceeds perceived value | Qualified prospects consistently decline paid pilot | Investigate scope and alternatives; test a narrower paid result before expanding |
| Members finish and leave | Good course completion but little paid continuation | Treat the program as episodic; redesign recurring utility before forecasting subscriptions |
| Broad role coverage exceeds expertise | Reviews delayed or assigned outside competence | Reserve expertise by role, show coverage honestly, and waitlist tailored services |
| Expert delivery consumes margin | Actual hours exceed reserved hours repeatedly | Revise future scope/pricing and fix process; honour existing commitments |
| AI feedback is misleading | Unsupported claims or rubric inconsistency | Source-linked feedback, bounded outputs, calibration, human appeal, and version rollback |
| Members cannot use templates at work | Employer environment or policy prevents use | Offer synthetic practice and permitted alternatives; verify compatibility before selling implementation |
| Sensitive information is uploaded | Client identifiers or restricted documents appear | Consent and rights checks, minimization, access controls, deletion, and incident procedure |
| Content grows stale | Broken workflows or obsolete domain assumptions | Version ownership, scheduled verification, visible readiness states |
| High-tier work becomes an agency | Scope expands into ongoing client delivery without capacity | Separate statement of work, delivery limits, acceptance, and staffing before sale |
| Customer acquisition depends on the founder | Sales stall without founder introductions | Test repeatable channels and document conversion cost; do not assume a marketplace effect |

Privacy, marketing consent, tax setup, cancellation terms, coaching agreements, intellectual-property permissions, and professional-liability coverage are concrete launch work. The evidence register provides official Canadian starting points. Local requirements must be confirmed for the actual business and customer locations.

## 16. Roadmap and release gates

Timings are planning ranges for a founder with consistent product attention, AI coding assistance, and available expert reviewers. They are not staffing or release commitments.

| Phase | Indicative timing | Result | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — Define and demonstrate | Weeks 1–2 | Finalized offer, common foundation, role briefs, sample assessment, expert roster, private prototype | Scope and service capacity are reviewable |
| B — Private MVP | Weeks 3–6 | Complete member/coach/operator journey with deterministic test adapters | Core acceptance journeys pass; no simulated result presented as live |
| C — First paid pilot | Weeks 7–14 | Five members, eight weeks of delivery, measured costs and assessed work | Actual launch prerequisites complete; progress and service quality tracked |
| D — Paid continuation | Weeks 15–20 | Continuation service, recurring billing operations, template maintenance and role coverage improvement | Paid continuation and viable delivery economics |
| E — Repeatability | Months 6–9 | Additional cohorts, customer support resilience, optional Specialist test | Repeatable acquisition, expert coverage, and useful continuing service |

A later phase can be prepared locally while an external dependency is unresolved. It cannot be marked commercially validated because the software exists. The detailed backlog specifies which MVP items Codex should implement now and which later features remain planned.

## 18. Business conclusion

The broad platform is technically implementable. Commercial viability rests on whether individual contractors buy and renew a coordinated expert service at the proposed prices, and whether that service can be delivered consistently across roles with adequate margin.

The immediate decision is to fund and validate the Professional pilot and private platform. The higher tiers preserve the user's desired annual-spend range while remaining conditional on stronger customer economics and a larger delivery obligation. Do not depend on CAD 120,000 subscriptions to make the initial business model work.
