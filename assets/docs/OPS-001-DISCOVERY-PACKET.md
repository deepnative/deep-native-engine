## What this experiment could learn

Deep Native Engine's shared learning and participation path serves IT practitioners, professionals in other fields and general learners. Optional career preparation and coached service are separate choices. The current [local learning slice](INITIAL-LEARNING-SLICE.md) uses synthetic examples and is neither a hosted product nor a reviewed six-lesson foundation. The [historical coaching catalog](CTP-002-ACCESS-OFFERS.md) is read-only and nonpurchasable; its prices are hypotheses, not a membership price or a buyer-facing quote.

Two questions must be measured separately:

1. **Learning and participation fit:** Which real tasks, difficulties, accessible formats and group activities would people choose, and what alternatives do they use now? This research must not imply that common foundation access is free, paid or unlimited; [#95](https://github.com/deepnative/deep-native-engine/issues/95) has only approved the no-purchase local-preview interim state.
2. **Optional coached-pilot fit:** For people who express a relevant need, would a specifically staffed, disclosed and separately approved service solve a problem they would pay to address? The archived 40 qualified prospects, 15 substantive conversations and five actual paid pilots are **planning targets for this optional experiment only**, not a forecast, conversion rate, ecosystem membership goal or permission to sell.

The experiment can be designed before outreach, but execution waits for the operator/markets and marketing-channel decision in [PLAN-002 #12](PLAN-002-LAUNCH-DECISIONS.md), qualified capacity in [PLAN-003 #13](PLAN-003-CAPACITY-OPERATIONS.md), reviewed sample/content status in [PLAN-004 #14](PLAN-004-CONTENT-PACK.md), approved offer/access terms in #95, and an explicit owner go/no-go for the named channel. A message, event invitation or referral request is not sent merely because this document exists.

## Truthful examples for a fit conversation

These are invented conversation prompts, not promised features, expert-graded work or published customer results. Show an actual reviewed sample only after its owner and readiness are verified.

| Audience | Example to discuss | What to ask, without assuming demand | Boundary to disclose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IT practitioner | With a fictional bug report, compare AI-suggested test cases against the source requirements and find one unsupported assumption. | “When did you last have to verify an AI-generated technical answer? What did you check, and what remained uncertain?” | The present exercise is synthetic; it is not production code review, certification or a guaranteed job outcome. |
| Professional in another field | With an invented operating procedure, compare an AI summary to the original text and identify a missing exception. | “Which recurring document or decision takes the most checking today? What happens when a summary is wrong?” | No client, employer, health or regulated material is requested. Domain-specific instruction and qualified review are not yet approved. |
| General or exploratory learner | With a fictional everyday question, try asking for sources, checking a claim and explaining what remains unknown. No coding or employment status is needed. | “What would make AI useful in a project you choose? What would help you feel safe checking the answer?” | The common path is a local preview. Its production access, price and limits remain undecided. |

For each conversation, first ask permission to discuss and record the person's own words in restricted storage. Do not ask for private work samples or copy their content into a public issue, model prompt, demo fixture or campaign report. If an example seems unsuitable, record that as a finding rather than steering the person to coaching.

## Discovery guide

Use the same core questions across audiences, then ask only the relevant optional-service questions. Avoid leading with a price or asserting a service is available.

1. “Tell me about the last time you tried to learn or apply AI to a real task. What did you try, and what happened?” Capture an observed recent event separately from an aspiration.
2. “What do you use instead today, and what does it cost in time, money or effort?” Record the named alternative and whether the cost is known, estimated or unknown.
3. “Which part would you want help with first: understanding, practice, checking an output, learning with others, or feedback on your own work?” Allow multiple choices and an open response.
4. “What would make this difficult to use?” Probe accessibility, time, language, privacy, source trust, unclear limits and group comfort without assuming a particular background.
5. “How would you tell that one exercise or project was useful?” Keep self-report, observed completion and qualified assessment distinct.
6. Only if the person raises high-touch help: “Have you paid for coaching or detailed review before? For what result? What would you need to know about scope, reviewer qualifications, schedule and price before considering it?” A hypothetical willingness-to-pay answer is not a purchase.
7. “If this did not fit, what is the main reason?” Record a person's own objection, including price, access, timing, trust, alternatives and no current need. Do not turn a polite response into a positive lead.

Close by asking whether the person wants any follow-up **only through an approved channel and permission process**. An interview does not imply consent to later marketing. Do not promise a cohort date, expert, reviewed curriculum, free tier, refund or access rights whose gate remains open.

## Channel and evidence controls before execution

The [archived channel list](context/source-2026-09-15/outputs/contractor-platform/01-business-plan.md) proposes founder relationships, voluntary referrals, permitted community participation, workshops, expert-led sessions, samples and possible partners. None is a current owned or authorized channel. A channel can move from `proposed` to `approved for a dated experiment` only after the owner records its actual operator, audience/market, permission basis, applicable terms, suppression process, approved copy, monitoring owner and stop date in a restricted operational record. No scraping, mass messaging, inferred association permission or assumed partner endorsement.

Keep personal contact and permission evidence out of this public repository. The restricted record should have one stable internal prospect key and, only if needed for the approved purpose, these fields:

| Field | Meaning and control |
| --- | --- |
| Source and permission | How the person entered the conversation; dated evidence of the exact channel/purpose permission, its owner and any scope or expiry. `Unknown` is not permission. |
| Suppression | Opt-out or do-not-contact state checked before any proposed follow-up. Preserve only the minimum needed to prevent another contact under the approved retention rule. |
| Audience and need | Self-described background(s), goal, recent task, alternative and access needs; overlapping tags do not create a paid tier or staff role. |
| Interaction state | `identified` → `permission verified` → `invited` → `substantive conversation` → `application` → `accepted terms` → `verified paid pilot`, each with an actual date/evidence pointer. Do not skip states or backfill success from a plan. |
| Offer and objection | Exact version and price actually shown **only if approved**, source of availability/capacity, answer in the person's words, decline reason and prior alternative. Unknown is distinct from zero. |
| Data stewardship | Restricted evidence location, collector, access list, deletion/retention decision, and correction/withdrawal request; no raw reply, CV or client material in GitHub. |

A channel owner must validate market-specific communication requirements and the operator's identity under [PLAN-002 D01/D02/D09](PLAN-002-LAUNCH-DECISIONS.md) before any invitation. This packet deliberately does not choose a legal basis, prescribe a universal retention period or provide send-ready campaign copy.

## Measures and stop rules

Report a dated funnel table with **actual** counts, denominator, source/channel and excluded records. Count a qualified coached prospect only after permission and the relevant need are recorded; count a substantive conversation only after the person discusses a recent problem and an alternative; count an application only after a deliberate request; count a paid pilot only after accepted versioned terms, verified payment and funded qualified capacity. Show 40/15/5 as the historical *target*, with actual numbers separate. A positive reply, workshop attendance, signed-up local preview, unverified manual receipt or simulation is not a paid pilot.

Report learning/participation interest separately: people who described a learning need, selected a format, wanted a circle, or tried a reviewed sample. Do not merge those denominators with coached-pilot prospects. Segment by self-described overlapping backgrounds and show `unknown`; segment totals may exceed the unique-person total. Suppress or aggregate small groups before sharing results outside the restricted team under an approved disclosure rule. Record no inferred employment, income, skill or demand outcome.

Pause the affected channel if permission evidence is absent, a suppression request arrives, a sample or price claim is inaccurate, the named operator/market is unresolved, or qualified capacity cannot support the proposed service. If people decline, record the actual alternatives and objections; do not call the experiment validated. A five-person cohort is directional evidence only, and Phase C/D remain gated by real delivery, usefulness, cost and continuation evidence.
