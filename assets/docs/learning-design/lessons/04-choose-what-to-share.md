# 4. Choose what to use and share

**You will leave with:** a useful invented sample and a short decision about its audience. Bring your checked answer from Lesson 3, or use a case below. Allow about 10–15 minutes. You do not need to upload anything or contact another person.

## See it

A good draft can still include information its next reader does not need. Before moving it into a tool or sharing it, make two decisions:

1. **Input:** what information does this task need, and may it be used in this place?
2. **Output:** who should see the result, and for what purpose?

Making a smaller sample can help you keep working. Removing a name, however, does not automatically give you permission to upload the rest. A distinctive quote, a private link or a credential may still be present. For this practice, use the invented sources provided here. The permission boundaries are part of each fictional case, not a new rule for your real workplace.

### Everyday — keep the useful planning facts

**Source E4:** A seed swap has six helpers, forty seed packets and one hour on Sunday. Location and start time are undecided. The organiser's private sheet also has each helper's phone number. The sheet is for organising helpers; no outside-tool upload or public release of that sheet is agreed.

**Task:** practise drafting a rough activity plan. A rough plan needs counts and time, not phone numbers.

**Worked sample:** “An invented seed swap has six helpers, forty packets and one hour on Sunday. Location and start time are undecided.”

**Decision:** keep that invented practice draft for yourself. If you later want to release a real invitation, confirm the actual details and the intended audience separately. The planning sample is useful without copying the contact sheet.

### Work — preserve the problem, replace the private material

**Source W4:** An internal note contains a client's name and a private complaint. Its useful process facts are: duplicate acknowledgments were reported; a team member will collect three examples by Thursday. The note is restricted to the internal team. Outside-tool use and publication are not agreed.

**Task:** practise turning notes into an action table.

**Worked sample:** “In an invented library team, Helper A will collect two examples of confusing shelf labels by Monday.”

**Decision:** use this newly written fictional case for practice. It retains the task/owner/date structure while changing the situation and its details. It is a new practice problem, not a faithful summary of the private note. Keep the original client's identity, quotation and private links out; simply changing a name in an otherwise copied complaint would not establish permission to use it elsewhere.

### Technical — keep the behavior, leave out the secret

**Source T4:** A private error log includes an access token, a customer's address and a form validation error. The relevant behavior is: a blank message should show an explanation. The log is restricted to the internal team; no external upload is agreed.

**Task:** practise describing a test case.

**Worked sample:** “Given a contact form with an invented email and a blank message, submitting should show an explanation that the message is required. Execution status: not run.”

**Decision:** create the sample from the stated behavior. Do not copy the token, the customer's address or the original log. A real token is not needed to describe this test. A draft test also does not prove the form works.

## Choose a useful next step

### Everyday question — `L4-E-P`

Which next step supports this practice task within E4's stated scope?

- **A.** Use the private sheet with names replaced by initials; keep the phone numbers.
- **B.** Write an invented brief with the counts, time and unknown details; keep it private.
- **C.** Publish the real invitation using the counts; leave out contacts and unknown details.

**Hint:** Removing a detail and changing the intended use are different decisions.

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**B keeps the useful planning facts** in an invented private exercise. **A still carries phone numbers** the rough plan does not need; initials do not remove those details. Leave the contact sheet behind. **C turns practice into a real public release** without establishing that audience or the real arrangements. Keep practising with the invented brief; confirm details and scope before making a real invitation.

</details>

### Work question — `L4-W-P`

You can read the internal note. Which statement follows from Source W4?

- **A.** Rename the client, then practise with the original complaint in an outside tool.
- **B.** Use a brief complaint excerpt; internal reading access covers the new tool.
- **C.** Create a fictional library-team note and practise a task/owner/date table from it.

**Hint:** Which option creates a new practice problem rather than inferring permission for the real note?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**C preserves the task structure** using a new fictional situation. **A changes a name but reuses the real complaint** in a place the case does not authorise; replace the situation and wording, not just the name. **B treats reading access as permission** for a different tool. A short excerpt still comes from the restricted note. Use original fictional facts while real-note reuse remains undecided.

</details>

### Technical question — `L4-T-P`

Every proposed sample will say execution is not run. Which input should you use in an outside drafting tool for the blank-message practice?

- **A.** An invented email and blank message, described from the stated behavior.
- **B.** The original log after removing token and customer address; no new permission needed.
- **C.** The customer's original email and blank message; the token is left out.

**Hint:** Does the sample need the original log or a real customer's input at all?

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**A creates the required practice case** from the behavior, without the original log or customer data. **B treats redaction as permission** for an outside tool; removing two fields does not change the log's stated restriction. Write a new fictional case instead. **C still uses the customer's address** when an invented email can demonstrate a blank message. Replace that address; removing the token alone does not make the real input necessary.

</details>

## Try a fresh case

For your route, write the sample you would use and identify its intended reader. Explain one detail you deliberately leave out.

| Case     | Fresh source and agreed scope                                                                                                                                                                   |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `L4-E-X` | A gardening group has ten helpers and meets on Saturday. The organiser agrees those two facts may appear in a public notice. Helpers' contact details must stay in the private organiser sheet. |
| `L4-W-X` | A team agrees its public update may say eighteen support requests were resolved. Individual request texts, names and internal links are excluded from that update.                              |
| `L4-T-X` | A maintainer asks for a fictional reproduction of an upload problem. The requirement is “PDF up to 2 MB.” Customer logs and credentials must not be included. No test has been run.             |

<details><summary>Compare your decision</summary>

- **Everyday:** “Ten helpers meet on Saturday” fits the notice's stated scope. Its audience is the public; phone numbers stay out. The scope does not let you add an unconfirmed address or time.
- **Work:** “Eighteen support requests were resolved” fits the agreed update. Its audience is the public; copied request text and internal links do not belong in it. You do not need those details to preserve the aggregate result.
- **Technical:** Describe an invented 3 MB PDF case with expected rejection under the 2 MB rule and status “not run.” Its reader is the maintainer requesting the fictional reproduction. No original logs, credentials or observed test result are needed.

Use the audience and scope explicitly given in your case. When the source permits a limited public statement, those facts can be used for that audience; when it names a particular recipient, keep that destination clear. The decision follows the supplied facts and destination.

</details>

## Make your own use-and-share note

Use your Lesson 3 artifact or an invented case above. Write:

> Purpose: …
>
> Useful facts I will keep: …
>
> Details I will replace or leave out: …
>
> Intended reader and purpose of sharing: …
>
> What establishes that scope, or what remains undecided: …

**Self-check:** the sample still supports the learning task; unnecessary identifying detail is absent; the audience is specific; and your stated permission does not exceed the source. If the real scope is uncertain, keep working with an original invented equivalent and leave the real sharing action undecided. Do not turn a private save into an implied request for review or publication.

**Take forward:** bring the sample and decision to [Lesson 5: Build a repeatable workflow](05-build-a-repeatable-workflow.md). You will decide when a draft is useful, when it needs repair and how to recover from missing information.
