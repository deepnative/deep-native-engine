# 6. Give feedback someone can use

**You will leave with:** a specific feedback note, a revised or retained draft and a reason for accepting, declining or already addressing the suggestion. Bring one part of your Lesson 5 workflow or use the examples below. Allow about 12–15 minutes. No actual message or submission is needed.

## See it

“This is bad” describes a reaction. Useful feedback helps someone do the next piece of work:

1. **Observation:** identify the exact statement or step you checked.
2. **Evidence:** point to the supplied source or criterion.
3. **Suggestion:** name one change or check.
4. **Limit:** say what you have not established.

The receiver then compares that advice with the source. Advice can be useful, mistaken or about an earlier draft. Accepting every suggestion is not the learning outcome; making a reasoned revision is.

### Everyday — turn a reaction into a useful correction

**Source E6:** The seed swap has six helpers and forty packets. The start time and location are undecided.

**Draft v1:** “Eight helpers will start at 10 a.m.”

**Weak feedback:** “Your plan is wrong.”

**Worked feedback:** “The draft says eight helpers and 10 a.m. E6 gives six helpers and no start time. Change the count to six and mark the time to be confirmed. I have checked only these supplied notes, not the event arrangements.”

**Revised v2:** “Six helpers are available; the start time is to be confirmed.”

**Revision reason:** “I matched the helper count and removed the assumed time.” Keep v1 and its feedback identifiable; the revision shows what changed without pretending the earlier draft said it all along.

### Work — disagree with a suggestion using evidence

**Source W6:** Mina drafts the survey. Arun checks the wording. The trial is Thursday. No survey deadline is agreed.

**Draft v1:** “Mina drafts the survey; deadline not specified.”

**Feedback:** “Change Mina's deadline to Thursday because the trial is Thursday.”

**Worked response:** “Thursday is the trial date. W6 does not give Mina's deadline, so I will keep it not specified. Is there another source where the deadline was agreed?”

**Recorded decision:** “Declined this change pending a source; retained the current wording.” This is a useful response even though the draft does not change. It keeps the known task and asks a precise clarification instead of inventing a commitment.

### Technical — recognise feedback that has already been addressed

**Source T6:** A blank message must show an explanation. No tests have been run. Maximum message length is unspecified.

**Draft v1:** “Blank-message test: passed. Maximum message length: 500.”

**Draft v2:** “Blank-message test: not run. Maximum message length: unspecified.”

**Feedback attached to v1:** “Remove the passing result and unsupported 500-character limit.”

**Worked response:** “Already addressed in v2: execution is not run and maximum length is unspecified. These match T6. The comment still refers to v1; v2 has not received a new review.”

This avoids making an unnecessary third edit or treating the old response as a review of new work.

## Choose the useful response

### Everyday question — `L6-E-P`

Which feedback would best help the writer repair E6's draft?

- **A.** “E6 says six helpers and leaves the start time open. Correct the count and mark the time to be confirmed; I have not checked the actual event.”
- **B.** “Make it more professional; I give it 100% once it sounds confident.”
- **C.** “Change the time to 9 a.m. because morning events work better.”

**Hint:** Look for an observed issue, source evidence and a concrete next action.

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**A is useful and bounded.** **B** substitutes tone and a score for evidence. **C** replaces one unsupported time with another. You can suggest a possible time as a separate planning idea, but you cannot label it an agreed fact from E6.

</details>

### Work question — `L6-W-P`

How should you handle the suggestion to give Mina a Thursday deadline?

- **A.** Accept it because feedback is always more authoritative than your own check.
- **B.** Delete Mina's task entirely because its deadline is uncertain.
- **C.** Keep the known task and unspecified deadline, explain the source distinction and ask whether a deadline source exists.

**Hint:** You can disagree with a change while still using the useful information.

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**C preserves the source and makes the next question clear.** **A** turns advice into a commitment without evidence. **B** throws away the stated task because one field is missing. A good decision can be “keep this wording, with a reason.”

</details>

### Technical question — `L6-T-P`

Feedback on v1 requests changes that already appear in v2. What should your revision record say?

- **A.** “The reviewer has approved v2,” because the requested changes are now present.
- **B.** “Already addressed in v2,” with the matching changes; retain the feedback's v1 scope.
- **C.** Rewrite v1 to look like v2, so there is no incorrect old answer.

**Hint:** A comment's source version and the current version are separate facts.

<details><summary>Check your answer</summary>

**B keeps the history truthful.** **A** invents a new review or approval. **C** removes the evidence of what was revised. Keep the earlier version identifiable and point to the newer correction; retention or deletion of real work follows the actual product's rules.

</details>

## Try a fresh case

Choose whether to **revise**, **ask/decline with a reason**, or **record already addressed**. Write the feedback response and name the source/version it concerns.

| Case | Fresh source, draft and feedback |
| --- | --- |
| `L6-E-X` | A reading group has nine people and forty-five minutes on Wednesday. The room is undecided. Draft v1 says nine people and an undecided room. Feedback says “make it twelve people in Room 4 so it sounds complete.” |
| `L6-W-X` | Noor is the assigned agenda owner; Lee invites participants. Draft v1 assigned the agenda to Lee. Draft v2 correctly assigns it to Noor. A comment on v1 says “fix the agenda owner.” |
| `L6-T-X` | A file form should accept PDFs up to 2 MB. No test has run. Draft v1 says “3 MB PDF rejection test: passed.” Feedback says “separate the expected rejection from the actual execution status.” |

<details><summary>Compare your response</summary>

- **Everyday:** ask/decline with a reason: “The source gives nine people and no room. I will keep those facts; do you have an updated source for twelve or Room 4?” Completeness of presentation does not establish new facts.
- **Work:** already addressed: “V2 assigns the agenda to Noor, matching the source. Your comment is on v1.” Do not claim v2 was reviewed merely because the correction is present.
- **Technical:** revise: “Expected result: reject a 3 MB PDF under the 2 MB rule. Execution: not run.” Reason: “The requirement supports the expectation, but no execution establishes a pass.”

Different wording is fine when the decision and reason follow the supplied evidence. If your answer simply says “done,” add the specific change or the reason you did not make one.

</details>

## Complete your feedback-and-revision record

Use an invented artifact from the course. Ask one focused question, such as “Does my recovery step preserve what remains unverified?” Then write a sample response yourself or compare it with a worked response above. Label it as practice feedback; do not invent a real reviewer.

Keep these pieces together:

> Source and draft version: …
>
> Focused question: …
>
> Feedback: observation + evidence + suggestion + limit.
>
> My decision: revise / ask or decline / already addressed.
>
> New text or retained text, with a reason: …

**Self-check rubric:** the comment identifies a specific issue; evidence supports its advice; the suggested action is usable; your decision has a reason; and the version history does not imply a review that never happened. If you edit the answer again, reassess any earlier self-check marks against the new text.

Keep your practice record for yourself unless you deliberately choose an audience. Asking for feedback from a particular person, joining a group and proposing a public reusable example are different actions. A private save or a helpful comment does not automatically make the work public.

**Take forward:** [complete the foundation practice](../foundation-practice.md) with one connected artifact. Keep what you can explain, mark what remains uncertain, and repeat the process with a fresh source.
