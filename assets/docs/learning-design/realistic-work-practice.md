**Capacity check:** 5 + (12 × 3) + 5 = 46 minutes, which exceeds thirty minutes by sixteen. For six ready cases, 5 + (6 × 3) + 5 = 28 minutes, leaving two minutes. This is a planning estimate, not a promise that every discussion will take exactly three minutes. A recommendation still needs a scope decision.

**One usable first update, before U1:**

> The handover pilot is now planned for Thursday, 11:00–11:30, remotely with three participants. Six of twelve case cards are ready, and Noor's one-page instruction draft has arrived. Using the three-minute sequential estimate plus opening and debrief, twelve cases need 46 minutes. I recommend using the six ready cards: 28 minutes on those assumptions, with two minutes spare. This keeps the session useful while the other cards await details; it still needs a scope decision. We also need a facilitator. Lee retains the four-comment task, but its deadline and the continuation-decision owner/date are open. Please resolve the scope and facilitator before the session. This update describes readiness and a proposed plan; it does not report pilot results.

**A reasonable alternative:** Five cases would take 25 minutes and leave five minutes for discussion. That can also be a defensible proposal if you explain why one ready case is held back and ask for the scope decision. Asking for a longer slot is another option, but the notes do not establish anyone's availability. The answer is judged by evidence, arithmetic and usefulness, not by matching six as a magic number.
