# The book swap: who is actually coming?

You are helping organise a neighbourhood book swap. An old invitation is still circulating, the room has changed, and the attendance list mixes confirmed guests with people waiting for a place. Write a reminder people can use and a short setup checklist. Then handle a later reply update without losing your first plan. This is an original fictional case; no real invitation, booking or purchase happens here.

## Brief summary

CAP-E2, packet revision 1: eighteen confirmed guests and two helpers fill a twenty-person room. The current booking is Saturday 7 November 2026, Cedar Room, with guests attending 1:30–3 p.m. Four people are waitlisted. Each confirmed guest may bring up to three books; two tables hold 48 books and the supplied holding box holds 20. Later replies change the attendance state. Counts are planning information, not a promise of turnout or book quantity.

## Flawed invitation to inspect

“See all 24 of you in Maple Room at 1 p.m. Bring any number of books—we will have exactly 54 to choose from. Everyone on the waitlist is in. The helpers can fit outside the headcount, and all books will fit on the tables.”

## Worked record

**1. Task and check.** Produce a usable reminder and setup checklist. Trace arrangements to S1/S2, add guests and helpers for the room count, and compare the maximum books with display/holding space. Keep proposed handling choices separate from confirmed arrangements.

**2. Request.** “Use CAP-E2 S0–S2 to draft a 100–160 word reminder for confirmed guests and a short internal checklist for Rae, Jo and Mina. Correct the old room/time, keep setup separate from guest arrival, and include the three-book limit and cancellation contact. Count all people in the room; do not admit the waitlist by implication. Calculate maximum books and propose a feasible display/box arrangement. Keep attendance and book totals uncertain. I will check each fact and calculation against the notes.”

**3. Claim check and repair.** S1 replaces the old room and times. In S1, **1 p.m. is setup access**, not guest arrival. Eighteen guests plus two helpers use all twenty places; the four waitlisted people are additional and unconfirmed. A maximum book count is not a promise that those books arrive.

| Check                        | Calculation | Meaning for the plan                                                          |
| ---------------------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| People with confirmed places | 18 + 2 = 20 | Room limit reached; the four waiting people have no place yet.                |
| Maximum incoming books       | 18 × 3 = 54 | Upper bound if every guest attends and brings three; actual count is unknown. |
| Table display space          | 2 × 24 = 48 | Six of the maximum 54 need another place initially.                           |
| Holding capacity             | 20 books    | Six overflow books fit; rotate them as display space opens.                   |

**One useful reminder:**

> Our book swap is on Saturday 7 November in Cedar Room, with guests attending from 1:30 to 3 p.m. Please use these details instead of the earlier Maple Room invitation. Your place is confirmed. You may bring up to three books, and bringing books is optional. If you can no longer come, please tell Rae so the attendance list can be updated. Places are limited, so please do not bring additional guests or forward this as an open invitation. Jo and Mina will arrange the books and rotate any held in the box onto the tables. The selection will depend on what people bring; we cannot promise a particular book. We look forward to seeing you.

**Internal checklist:** Rae checks the eighteen confirmed guests, two helpers and four waiting people as separate groups before sending the reminder. Jo and Mina enter at 1 p.m., arrange the two tables and supplied box, welcome confirmed guests at 1:30, close the swap at 3 and finish packing by 3:30. This describes their plan, not completed actions. Track the actual incoming books on the day.

A defensible alternative is to display up to two books per guest first: at most **36 books** on the tables and **18 books** in the box, then rotate. Both fit the supplied capacities. The three-book guest limit stays unchanged. Choose a practical handling plan and explain it; copying the example's exact wording is unnecessary.

**4. Use/share decision.** The reminder gives confirmed guests the corrected arrangements and useful instructions. Rae's internal checklist retains pending/confirmed/waitlist distinctions; guests do not need other people's reply records. Keep both as fictional practice drafts here. No message is sent by saving or downloading them.

**5. Repeatable workflow.** Keep source versions → separate arrangement changes from attendance replies → draft the reminder and checklist → check room count, book capacity and each claim → keep the checked draft with its date → revise when a new source arrives. If the booking note is unavailable, recover it before confirming room/time; retain the useful draft and mark those facts unverified meanwhile. If a writing tool fails, use the retained notes to write the short reminder manually.

**6. Practice feedback and revision.** A useful comment on the flawed invitation is: “The room limit includes helpers, and the waitlist has no confirmed places. Use Cedar Room and the 1:30 guest time. Treat 54 books as a maximum and plan where overflow goes.” This is supplied practice feedback on the fictional invitation, not a review of your draft.

That is **17 + 2 = 19 confirmed people** in the room, plus **one reserved place**. If the pending person accepts, the total becomes twenty. The seventeen confirmed guests could bring at most 51 books; allow for up to 54 if the pending place becomes confirmed. Actual turnout and book quantities remain unknown. The two-books-first alternative needs at most 34 displayed and 17 held now, or 36 displayed and 18 held if the pending person joins.

The reminder's room, times and book limit remain correct. A learner may retain that exact text and record a revised checklist plus reason. If writing a separate reminder to the newly accepted guest, this is also usable:

> Your place at Saturday's book swap is now confirmed. Please come to Cedar Room on 7 November from 1:30 to 3 p.m.; the earlier Maple Room details have changed. You may bring up to three books, or come without books. Please let Rae know if you can no longer attend. The room has limited places, so this confirmation does not include additional guests. Jo and Mina will organise the display and bring out books from the holding box as space becomes available. What is available will depend on what people bring. Please use this reminder for the current arrangements and avoid circulating the old invitation. We look forward to welcoming you.

**Revision reason:** “Two cancellations and one accepted replacement changed confirmed guests from eighteen to seventeen. The other invitation is still pending, with one place reserved. I updated the internal count and recipient list, while retaining the still-correct room, time and book limit. I kept the first version so the change is explainable.”

**Next action:** at or after Friday 6 p.m., Rae checks the actual replies and current list before offering the held place to the next person. Neither the passage of time nor silence proves an acceptance. If reply access is unavailable, keep the place status unresolved until Rae can check. A suggestion to “say everyone is confirmed to make the invitation simpler” would erase a meaningful distinction; a concise reminder can omit internal counts while the checklist stays accurate.
